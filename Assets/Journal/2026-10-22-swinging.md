# Swinging

One of the problems with deforming a model with force from a line that projects perfectly forward from the camera is that it dents it in at just the perspective that it sort of looks shaded differently. Let me take a screenshot...

![a caved in face I guess](./Textures/dent.png)

I can tell this is dented I guess, but it doesn't really make the face look deformed. If I was punching a head I'd be swinging at it from an offset, not shooting a perfectly straight force beam from my eyes, even though that would be very cool.

I added two offsets, one for a right "fist" and one for a left, stored as `rightFist` and `leftFist` in `MeshDeformerInput` (the RightOffset and LeftOffset children of Main Camera). Since they're parented to the camera, they move with it and always sit off to either side of my view.

The target is the same as before: the spot on the head under the cursor. What's different is the origin. The force used to start at the camera, and now it starts at one of the fists. The script picks the fist on the same side of the screen as the cursor:

```csharp
GameObject fist = Input.mousePosition.x > Screen.width / 2f ? rightFist : leftFist;
Vector3 punchDirection = (hit.point - fist.transform.position).normalized;
```

`punchDirection` runs from the fist to the target. Both the dent and the impulse on the Rigidbody use it now instead of `inputRay.direction`, which ran from the camera to the target. So clicking the right side of the face drives the dent in at an angle from the right, and the head gets knocked the same way, which reads more like a swing than a beam out of my eyes.

To change how angled the punches are, I just move the offsets. Further out to the sides gives more of a hook, and closer to the camera gets back toward the straight-on dent.

## Force vs. impulse

`MeshDeformerInput` has two strength settings, and they control different things.

- **`force`** sets how deep the dent is. It gets passed to `MeshDeformer.AddDent`, which multiplies it by the deformer's `depth`. At `force = 2` the dent is twice as deep, but never deeper than `maxDepth`. It only reshapes the mesh and doesn't move the head at all.
- **`impulse`** sets how hard the head gets knocked. It's a physics push applied to the head's non-kinematic Rigidbody at the spot where the punch lands, using `ForceMode.Impulse`, so it's a single instant shove rather than a steady push. Because it's applied at the impact point and not at the center, hitting off-center makes the head rock and spin. It doesn't change the mesh's shape at all.

Both use the same `punchDirection`, so the dent and the knock come from the same fist. They can be tuned separately though: a high `force` and low `impulse` gives deep dents in a head that barely moves, and the other way around gives a head that swings a lot but hardly dents.
