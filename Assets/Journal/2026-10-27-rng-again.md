# Randomness Again

After thinking about it for a few days, I think it's a mistake to have the numbers be purely random. I have no idea how they should be affected though. My intuition tells me to not overthink it and instead to just make any values I can find in inside MeshDeformer or MeshDeformerInput public and then map or constrain them to normal D&D values. That way I'm just making a system that is already more complex than I totally understand visible and then playing with that, which is a way I like to work, because I'm not of an engineering mindset, I'm a person who likes to experiment with something that is already there while trying to build a mental model of it.

Also if I just make the values totally random, what am I even doing in this project? I would have just taken a tutorial I found online and made a quick joke out of it, which isn't worthy of journaling. I'd like to seek out and engage with some complexity, even if the result is very simple.

The main thing in MeshDefomer that changes is displacedVertices though, and that's an array of potentially hundreds of Vec3's that represent mesh vertice adjustments. I don't really know what to do with that exactly. I can't just grab three five numbers that change with every punch and make them into ints between 0 and 20. I have to somehow turn them into regions and then average those regions into adjustment ints that sort of make sense. Meaning that I can't just wing it I do have to kind of consider what I'm doing from the ground up, the ground being a bunch of mesh vertices that are positions on a head.

Today I really was feeling how rusty I am with Unity. It took me like 30 minutes to remember how to do canvas layout groups again for TextMeshPro objects.
